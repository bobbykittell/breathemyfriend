Step 2 · Mind

Observe your thoughts

You are not your thoughts. You're the one watching them.

In one line

A thought is a guess your brain makes. You can watch it arrive, thank it, and let it go, without obeying it.

Your brain is a weather forecaster

Your brain's main job is predicting what happens next, so it can get your body ready in time. Every worried thought is a forecast. Some forecasts are right. Many aren't.

The forecast

"This meeting is going to go badly."

What the body does

Heart speeds up, stomach tightens, getting ready for trouble.

What actually happens

Usually: the meeting is fine. The storm never came.

A forecaster who's been through hard years gets cautious and predicts storms a lot. That's not a broken brain. It's a brain that learned to protect you. It just needs new information: you're safe now.

The 90-second wave

Neuroscientist Jill Bolte Taylor noticed that the chemical rush of a feeling tends to rise and fall in about 90 seconds. What keeps it going longer is the story we keep telling about it.

Three moves

Use these whenever an anxious thought shows up.

1

Notice it

Catch the thought as a thought. Name it.

"There's the worry about money again."

2

Honor it

Don't fight it. It showed up to protect you. Thank it.

"Thanks, brain. I know you're trying to keep me safe."

3

Let it pass

Feel it in the body. Breathe low and slow. Watch it float off.

"I don't need to solve this right now."

Try this: put some space in front of it

Add a few words in front of an anxious thought and notice how it loosens its grip.

"I'm going to fail."
"I'm having the thought that I'm going to fail."
"I notice I'm having the thought that I'm going to fail."

Same thought. But now there's a gap between you and it. In that gap, you get to choose.

If it comes back

That's normal

Thoughts return. That's not failing. Every return is another chance to practice watching.

Picture clouds

You're the sky. Thoughts are clouds. Some are dark and heavy. All of them move. The sky is never harmed.

Body first, again

If a thought keeps hooking you, go back to Step 1. Breathe less, stretch, shake. A calmer body makes fewer storm forecasts.
